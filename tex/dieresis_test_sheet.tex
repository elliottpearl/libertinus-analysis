\subsection*{1. Composed against precomposed, in numbers}
For every lowercase precomposed letter of a Latin base with one above mark, the mark's position relative to the base is compared:
composed (base + CGJ + mark, shaped by HarfBuzz) minus precomposed (the designer's glyph, found by matching the outlines of the base and of the mark).
$\Delta x$ is measured along the slant; positive means the composed mark lands right of the designer's.
Median over the letters (their number in brackets); ď ľ ť (comma caron) excluded.
$k$ is the mark's own shift (the centre of its outline on its axis minus its anchor X): 0 in the anchor model, 150 in semibold italic (K).

\medskip
In semibold italic every mark lands 5--16 right of the precomposed glyphs except the dieresis, which lands 26 left:
\textbf{in the precomposed glyphs the dieresis sits where the other marks sit, about 40 right of where its anchor puts it.}
That is its $k$: 112 against 150.
The macron's $k$ is 141, and its composed position is within 5 of the precomposed one (ink centres; the precomposed macron is drawn 32 higher).
This contradicts the to-do item's ``the precomposed glyphs do not support moving the dieresis on lowercase''.

In the other styles: regular agrees with its precomposed glyphs throughout (within 10, except the grave);
semibold's dieresis ($k = 12$) lands 13.5 right of its precomposed glyphs, the other way;
italic's circumflex and tilde land 18 and 24 right, and its dieresis is 12--21 left of the circumflex relative to the precomposed glyphs on a, e, w.

\begin{description}

Trial fonts B and C are the patched semibold italic with only that anchor changed.

IPA centralized vowels mix precomposed letters (ä ë ö ü ï) with composed ones (ɛ̈ ɘ̈ ə̈ ɔ̈ ʊ̈ ɪ̈ ɐ̈ ʌ̈), so a dieresis off the precomposed position shows within one transcription.
Same for the macron (ā ē ō ū against ɛ̄ ə̄ ɔ̄ ʊ̄).
