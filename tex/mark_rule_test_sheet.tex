\section*{Test sheet: the rule for symmetric above marks, and the vertical evidence}

\textbf{Proposed rule.} A symmetric above mark (circumflex, tilde, macron, breve, dot above, dieresis, ring, caron) has its anchor on its own axis:
$k = K$, where $k$ is the centre of the mark's outline on its axis minus its anchor X, and $K$ is 0 (150 in semibold italic).
It is applied only where $|k - K| \ge 10$; smaller deviations are within the designer's own spread.
Asymmetric marks (grave, acute, double acute, double grave) keep their designed $k$.
In the patched fonts this moves four mark anchors:

\begin{itemize}
\item Italic \char"25CC\char"0303\ U+0303: $k = 11$; anchor $(-17, 697) \to (-6, 697)$, so the mark moves 11 left.
\item Semibold \char"25CC\char"0308\ U+0308: $k = 12$; anchor $(-198, 653) \to (-186, 653)$, so the mark moves 12 left.
\item Semibold italic \char"25CC\char"0303\ U+0303: $k = 139$; anchor $(-134, 700) \to (-145, 700)$, so the mark moves 11 right.
\item Semibold italic \char"25CC\char"0308\ U+0308: $k = 112$; anchor $(-210, 645) \to (-248, 645)$, so the mark moves 38 right.
\end{itemize}
The ``rule'' fonts below are the patched fonts with only these anchors changed.

\subsection*{1. Horizontal: composed minus precomposed, now and under the rule}
$\Delta x$: median over the lowercase precomposed letters of the mark's offset, composed (base + CGJ + mark) minus the designer's precomposed glyph, along the slant;
positive means the composed mark lands right of the designer's.
Under the rule $\Delta x$ is no longer zeroed per mark: what remains is common to all the marks of a style (about $+12$ to $+16$ in semibold italic, where the designer builds the precomposed glyphs with the base about 12 right), which is a question for the base anchors, not the marks.

\subsection*{2. Vertical: the evidence of $\Delta y$}
$\Delta y$: the same comparison vertically; positive means the composed mark sits higher than the designer's.
Median over the precomposed letters with an x-height base (ascender letters such as ḃ ḋ ḣ ḱ excluded: the designer puts the mark beside the ascender), with the range.
On x-height bases the base anchor is at the reference height (645) for every letter, so a $\Delta y$ that is the same on every base belongs to the mark's anchor: it is evidence for moving that anchor down (positive $\Delta y$) or up (negative).
A spread across bases (italic macron: $-1$ on ā ē ḡ, $-32$ on ī ō ū ȳ) means the designer's precomposed glyphs disagree among themselves.
Bold: $|\Delta y| \ge 10$.
